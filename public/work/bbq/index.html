Post Oak & Pepper · Fort Worth barbecue

Fourteen hours from first fire to first slice

The pit waits at dusk. Scroll one overnight cook on post oak, then price catering for your crew.

9:00 to 9:45 pm

We burn post oak and nothing else

Split and stacked to season for a year, so it burns clean. Once the startup smoke clears, a hot fire with plenty of air sends thin blue smoke out of the stack.

9:45 to 10:00 pm

Salt and coarse pepper, half and half

A fourteen-pound prime brisket goes on at ten. Tap a pin for the details.

10:00 pm to 6:20 am

The bark sets, a little more every hour

Around 160° the meat stops climbing for hours while moisture cools the surface. That is the stall, and it is normal.

6:20 am, sunrise

Wrapping in pink paper at first light

Butcher paper breathes, so the bark holds its crust instead of steaming soft like it would in foil. Off when a probe slides in like butter, around 203°.

11:00 am

Rested two hours, then sliced

Off the pit at nine and held warm until the doors open. The pink ring under the bark is the smoke.

Pitmaster's notes

Book a week out
A week for most events, two weeks for anything over 100 guests.
Why it takes all night
A whole brisket cooks low, around 275°, for about eleven hours, then rests for two. Nearly three hours of the cook is the stall, when moisture on the surface cools the meat and the probe barely moves.
Whole briskets
Yes, by the pound or whole, sliced or unsliced.
The pit at your place
For events of 75 guests or more we tow the pit out with a pitmaster. It is in the estimator.
Leftovers
They come with reheating notes. There are rarely leftovers.
