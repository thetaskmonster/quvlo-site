Questions we get

How do I water new sod in a Dallas summer?

Little and often until it roots: enough to keep the soil under the sod moist, which can mean every day when it's over 80°F. Once it has rooted, you switch to deep, infrequent watering. Dallas allows sprinklers at most two days a week and never between 10 a.m. and 6 p.m., so for new sod we apply for the city's new-landscape variance, which allows extra watering days for up to five weeks. Hand-watering is allowed any day. Other DFW cities set their own days, and we check yours.

When does the first mow happen?

Mow once the sod has rooted and is being watered less often; in warm weather that is usually two to three weeks. If a corner holds when you tug it, it's rooted. Warm-season sod laid late in the fall may not root until spring, so the first cut waits until then.

Do you haul everything away?

Yes. Chips, brush, broken concrete and old fence all leave on our trailers. You will not find a pile behind the shed.

What if it rains during the job?

Clay can't be graded wet, so we stop and pick back up once the ground firms up. The price stays the same; only the dates move.

Can you handle the HOA?

We draw the plan your HOA asks for and submit it with you before any work starts.

Can I choose my mowing pattern?

On a weekly program, yes. Straight, diagonal, checkerboard or diamond, rotated through the season so the grass stays healthy.
